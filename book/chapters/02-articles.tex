\gpart{III}{Articles \& Case}

% ------------------------------------------------------------
\gsec{The four cases at a glance}

\gintro{English shows who does what to whom mainly through word order. German
does it through case: the article, and sometimes the noun, changes shape
according to the job the noun is doing. There are four cases.}

\begin{gtbl}{colspec={X[0.85,l] X[1.35,l] X[0.75,l] X[1.5,l]}}
  Case & Job in the sentence & Asks & Example \\
  Nominativ & subject & wer? was? & \textbf{Der Hund} beißt den Mann. \\
  Akkusativ & direct object & wen? was? & Der Hund beißt \textbf{den Mann}. \\
  Dativ & indirect object & wem? & Ich gebe \textbf{dem Mann} das Buch. \\
  Genitiv & possessor & wessen? & das Auto \textbf{des Mannes} \\
\end{gtbl}

\begin{gnote}
In the examples above the two nouns swap case rather than position:
\textit{Den Mann beißt der Hund} still means the dog does the biting. Because
the endings carry that information, German word order is freer than English.
\end{gnote}

\tcap{The definite article across all four cases}

\begin{gendertbl}{}
  Definite & maskulin & feminin & neutrum & Plural \\
  Nominativ & der & die & das & die \\
  Akkusativ & \en{den} & die & das & die \\
  Dativ     & \en{dem} & \en{der} & \en{dem} & \en{den}\,+n \\
  Genitiv   & \en{des}\,+s & \en{der} & \en{des}\,+s & \en{der} \\
\end{gendertbl}

\begin{gnote}
The \textit{+n} and \textit{+s} marks in the last two rows are endings that go
on the noun rather than the article; both are explained on their own pages.
\end{gnote}

% ------------------------------------------------------------
\gsec{Nominative}

\gintro{The nominative marks the subject — whoever or whatever is performing the
verb. It is also the form used after \textit{sein}, \textit{werden},
\textit{bleiben} and \textit{heißen}, and the form dictionaries list.}

\begin{posstbl}{}
  Group & Form & maskulin & feminin & neutrum & Plural \\
  \SetCell[r=3]{c,m} Articles & the   & der  & die   & das  & die   \\
                              & a     & ein  & eine  & ein  & \xx   \\
                              & not a & kein & keine & kein & keine \\
  \hline\hline
  \SetCell[r=5]{c,m} Singular & my \eng{1st}      & mein   & meine  & mein   & meine \\
                              & your \eng{2nd}    & dein   & deine  & dein   & deine \\
                              & his \eng{3rd m}   & sein   & seine  & sein   & seine \\
                              & her \eng{3rd f}   & ihr    & ihre   & ihr    & ihre \\
                              & its \eng{3rd n}   & sein   & seine  & sein   & seine \\
  \SetCell[r=3]{c,m} Plural   & our \eng{1st}     & unser  & unsere & unser  & unsere \\
                              & your \eng{2nd}    & euer   & eure   & euer   & eure \\
                              & their \eng{3rd}   & ihr    & ihre   & ihr    & ihre \\
  Formal                      & your \eng{2nd}    & Ihr    & Ihre   & Ihr    & Ihre \\
\end{posstbl}

\begin{gnote}
Every row below the double rule carries the same endings as \textit{ein}:
nothing, \textit{-e}, nothing, \textit{-e}. The possessives differ from one
another only in their stem. \textit{euer} drops its second \textit{e} whenever
an ending follows, giving \textit{eure} rather than \textit{euere}.
\end{gnote}

\tcap{Examples}

\begin{extbl}{colspec={X[1,l] X[1.6,l]}}
  \textbf{Der} Hund schläft. & The dog is sleeping. \\
  \textbf{Meine} Schwester heißt Anna. & My sister is called Anna. \\
  Das ist \textbf{ein} gutes Buch. & That is a good book. \\
  \textbf{Kein} Mensch weiß das. & Nobody knows that. \\
\end{extbl}

\gsec{Accusative}

\gintro{The accusative marks the direct object — the thing the verb acts on. It
also follows the accusative prepositions and measures spans of time.}

\begin{posstbl}{}
  Group & Form & maskulin & feminin & neutrum & Plural \\
  \SetCell[r=3]{c,m} Articles & the   & \en{den}    & die   & das  & die   \\
                              & a     & \en{einen}  & eine  & ein  & \xx   \\
                              & not a & \en{keinen} & keine & kein & keine \\
  \hline\hline
  \SetCell[r=5]{c,m} Singular & my \eng{1st}      & \en{meinen}  & meine        & mein         & meine \\
                              & your \eng{2nd}    & \en{deinen}  & deine        & dein         & deine \\
                              & his \eng{3rd m}   & \en{seinen}  & seine        & sein         & seine \\
                              & her \eng{3rd f}   & \en{ihren}   & ihre         & ihr          & ihre \\
                              & its \eng{3rd n}   & \en{seinen}  & seine        & sein         & seine \\
  \SetCell[r=3]{c,m} Plural   & our \eng{1st}     & \en{unseren} & unsere       & unser        & unsere \\
                              & your \eng{2nd}    & \en{euren}   & eure         & euer         & eure \\
                              & their \eng{3rd}   & \en{ihren}   & ihre         & ihr          & ihre \\
  Formal                      & your \eng{2nd}    & \en{Ihren}   & Ihre         & Ihr          & Ihre \\
\end{posstbl}

\begin{gnote}
Only the masculine column changes. The feminine, neuter and plural columns are
letter-for-letter identical to the nominative page, which means a sentence
containing no masculine singular noun looks the same in either case.
\end{gnote}

\tcap{Examples}

\begin{extbl}{colspec={X[1,l] X[1.6,l]}}
  Ich sehe \textbf{den} Hund. & I see the dog. \\
  Sie kauft \textbf{einen} Tisch. & She is buying a table. \\
  Wir haben \textbf{keinen} Hunger. & We are not hungry. \\
  Er liest \textbf{seine} Zeitung. & He is reading his newspaper. \\
\end{extbl}

\begin{gnote}
The accusative also expresses duration and definite time without any
preposition: \textit{Ich bleibe \textbf{einen Monat}}, \textit{Er kommt
\textbf{jeden Tag}}, \textit{\textbf{Nächste Woche} fahre ich weg}.
\end{gnote}

% ------------------------------------------------------------
\gsec{Dative}

\gintro{The dative marks the indirect object — the person something is given to
or done for. It also follows the dative prepositions and a group of verbs whose
only object is dative.}

\begin{posstbl}{}
  Group & Form & maskulin & feminin & neutrum & Plural \\
  \SetCell[r=3]{c,m} Articles & the   & \en{dem}    & \en{der}    & \en{dem}    & \en{den}    \\
                              & a     & \en{einem}  & \en{einer}  & \en{einem}  & \xx         \\
                              & not a & \en{keinem} & \en{keiner} & \en{keinem} & \en{keinen} \\
  \hline\hline
  \SetCell[r=5]{c,m} Singular & my \eng{1st}      & \en{meinem}  & \en{meiner}  & \en{meinem}  & \en{meinen} \\
                              & your \eng{2nd}    & \en{deinem}  & \en{deiner}  & \en{deinem}  & \en{deinen} \\
                              & his \eng{3rd m}   & \en{seinem}  & \en{seiner}  & \en{seinem}  & \en{seinen} \\
                              & her \eng{3rd f}   & \en{ihrem}   & \en{ihrer}   & \en{ihrem}   & \en{ihren} \\
                              & its \eng{3rd n}   & \en{seinem}  & \en{seiner}  & \en{seinem}  & \en{seinen} \\
  \SetCell[r=3]{c,m} Plural   & our \eng{1st}     & \en{unserem} & \en{unserer} & \en{unserem} & \en{unseren} \\
                              & your \eng{2nd}    & \en{eurem}   & \en{eurer}   & \en{eurem}   & \en{euren} \\
                              & their \eng{3rd}   & \en{ihrem}   & \en{ihrer}   & \en{ihrem}   & \en{ihren} \\
  Formal                      & your \eng{2nd}    & \en{Ihrem}   & \en{Ihrer}   & \en{Ihrem}   & \en{Ihren} \\
\end{posstbl}

\begin{gnote}
The endings run \textit{-m, -r, -m, -n} across every row. Masculine and neuter
are identical throughout the dative, which removes one decision entirely.
\end{gnote}

\begin{gnote}
The dative plural also adds \textit{-n} to the noun:
\textit{die Kinder} $\rightarrow$ \textit{mit den Kinder\textbf{n}};
\textit{die Bücher} $\rightarrow$ \textit{in den Bücher\textbf{n}}. Plurals
already ending in \textit{-n} or \textit{-s} are left alone
(\textit{den Autos}).
\end{gnote}

\tcap{Examples}

\begin{extbl}{colspec={X[1,l] X[1.4,l]}}
  Ich gebe \textbf{dem} Kind einen Ball. & I give the child a ball. \\
  Sie hilft \textbf{ihrer} Mutter. & She helps her mother. \\
  Wir fahren mit \textbf{dem} Zug. & We are going by train. \\
  Das gehört \textbf{meinen} Eltern. & That belongs to my parents. \\
\end{extbl}

\gsec{Genitive}

\gintro{The genitive marks possession, corresponding to English \emph{'s} or
\emph{of}. It also follows the genitive prepositions. In speech it is commonly
replaced by \textit{von + Dativ}, but it remains standard in writing.}

\begin{posstbl}{}
  Group & Form & maskulin & feminin & neutrum & Plural \\
  \SetCell[r=3]{c,m} Articles & the   & \en{des}    & \en{der}    & \en{des}    & \en{der}    \\
                              & a     & \en{eines}  & \en{einer}  & \en{eines}  & \xx         \\
                              & not a & \en{keines} & \en{keiner} & \en{keines} & \en{keiner} \\
  \hline\hline
  \SetCell[r=5]{c,m} Singular & my \eng{1st}      & \en{meines}  & \en{meiner}  & \en{meines}  & \en{meiner} \\
                              & your \eng{2nd}    & \en{deines}  & \en{deiner}  & \en{deines}  & \en{deiner} \\
                              & his \eng{3rd m}   & \en{seines}  & \en{seiner}  & \en{seines}  & \en{seiner} \\
                              & her \eng{3rd f}   & \en{ihres}   & \en{ihrer}   & \en{ihres}   & \en{ihrer} \\
                              & its \eng{3rd n}   & \en{seines}  & \en{seiner}  & \en{seines}  & \en{seiner} \\
  \SetCell[r=3]{c,m} Plural   & our \eng{1st}     & \en{unseres} & \en{unserer} & \en{unseres} & \en{unserer} \\
                              & your \eng{2nd}    & \en{eures}   & \en{eurer}   & \en{eures}   & \en{eurer} \\
                              & their \eng{3rd}   & \en{ihres}   & \en{ihrer}   & \en{ihres}   & \en{ihrer} \\
  Formal                      & your \eng{2nd}    & \en{Ihres}   & \en{Ihrer}   & \en{Ihres}   & \en{Ihrer} \\
\end{posstbl}

\begin{gnote}
The endings run \textit{-s, -r, -s, -r}. Masculine and neuter match each other,
as do feminine and plural. The noun takes an ending too: \textit{-es} after one
syllable or after \textit{s, ß, z, tz, x} (\textit{des Kind\textbf{es}},
\textit{des Hau\textbf{ses}}), otherwise \textit{-s}
(\textit{des Lehrer\textbf{s}}).
\end{gnote}

\tcap{Examples}

\begin{extbl}{colspec={X[1,l] X[1.3,l]}}
  das Auto \textbf{des} Mannes & the man's car \\
  die Farbe \textbf{der} Tür & the colour of the door \\
  wegen \textbf{des} schlechten Wetters & because of the bad weather \\
  trotz \textbf{ihrer} Probleme & despite her problems \\
\end{extbl}

\gsec{der-words and ein-words}

\gintro{Every determiner belongs to one of two families. Which family a word is
in determines both its own ending and the adjective ending that follows it, so
it is worth being able to recognise them.}

\tcap{der-words, which take the definite-article endings}

\begin{flattbl}{colspec={X[0.85,l] X[1,l] X[0.85,l] X[1.1,l]}, vline{3}={1-Z}{0.5pt,solid,Line}, column{3}={font=\small\sffamily\bfseries, fg=Ink}}
  dieser  & this            & jeder    & each, every \eng{sg} \\
  jener   & that \eng{lit.} & mancher  & some, many a \\
  welcher & which           & solcher  & such \\
  aller   & all             & derselbe & the same \\
\end{flattbl}

\begin{gendertbl}{}
  dieser & maskulin & feminin & neutrum & Plural \\
  Nominativ & dieser & diese  & dieses & diese  \\
  Akkusativ & diesen & diese  & dieses & diese  \\
  Dativ     & diesem & dieser & diesem & diesen \\
  Genitiv   & dieses & dieser & dieses & dieser \\
\end{gendertbl}

\tcap{ein-words, which have no ending in three slots}

\begin{flattbl}{colspec={X[0.85,l] X[2.15,l]}}
  Members & ein, kein, and every possessive: mein, dein, sein, ihr, unser, euer, Ihr \\
\end{flattbl}

\begin{gnote}
The two families differ in exactly three cells: masculine nominative, and neuter
nominative and accusative. There a der-word shows \textit{-er} or \textit{-es}
while an ein-word shows nothing at all — \textit{dies\textbf{er} Mann} beside
\textit{ein Mann}. In the other thirteen cells the endings are the same.
\end{gnote}
